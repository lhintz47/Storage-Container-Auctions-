FND‑01

Why not Kickstarter

Worth ruling out explicitly before picking a path. Kickstarter and Indiegogo are reward-based crowdfunding: backers pledge money and receive a product or perk in return, not a financial stake. That model fits a single product with something to ship — it doesn't fit an operating business with physical facilities, ongoing revenue, and investors who'd reasonably want a return tied to that revenue. Kickstarter's own rules explicitly exclude offering equity, revenue share, or financial returns as a reward.

The real fit for "fund an operating small business from the public" is Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) — a specific SEC exemption built for exactly this, letting a private company raise real capital from both accredited and non-accredited investors online, in exchange for either equity or a revenue-share note. That's the path this plan builds around.

FND‑02

Legal framework

Reg CF is a real securities offering, not a marketing campaign with a legal footnote — treat it that way from the start.

  • Raise cap: up to $5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period.
  • Must go through an SEC-registered intermediary — either a funding portal (Wefunder, StartEngine, Republic, Mainvest) or a broker-dealer. No direct-to-investor raise outside one of these.
  • Open to non-accredited investors, not just wealthy/institutional ones — this is what makes it genuinely "crowd" funding, unlike most other securities exemptions.
  • Form C filing with the SEC is required to launch an offering, and an annual report stays required for as long as the securities are outstanding — an ongoing compliance obligation, not a one-time filing.

Financial statement tiers

Compliance cost scales directly with how much is raised — this is the number that should drive the funding target (Sec. 05), not the other way around.

  • Up to $124,000Financial statements self-certified by the company's principal executive officer — no CPA required. The cheapest tier by far.
  • $124,001–$618,000CPA-reviewed financial statements required.
  • Over $618,000Full CPA-audited financial statements required.
  • First-time issuer exceptionA company's first Reg CF raise can use reviewed (not audited) financials up to $1,235,000 — a meaningfully cheaper compliance path than the standard Tier 3 audit requirement, but only available once.
This needs a securities attorney, not just a pitch deck — Form C disclosure requirements, choosing a security structure, and ongoing reporting obligations are real legal work with real liability if done wrong. Budget for it as a line item (Sec. 05), not an afterthought.
FND‑03

Equity vs. revenue-share

Both are legitimate under Reg CF and both are on the table — the tradeoffs are real and worth deciding deliberately, not defaulting into whichever platform is most famous.

Revenue-share note

  • Investors get a cut of actual revenue until a target return is hit (e.g. 1.5× invested capital over 3–5 years) — no ownership stake, no dilution.
  • Ties investor return directly to the business actually generating revenue (buyer's premium + seller commission, already defined in the business plan) rather than to a future acquisition or IPO that may never happen.
  • Natural fit for a cash-flowing physical small business — this is literally the model Mainvest built its platform around (restaurants, breweries, local real estate).
  • Downside: payout obligations continue regardless of how well other parts of the business are doing, until the note is satisfied.

Equity

  • Investors buy real ownership shares — familiar structure, appeals to investors looking for long-term upside if the company grows and is eventually sold or scales significantly.
  • Larger, more established platforms (Wefunder, StartEngine) and larger 2024 raise volumes (Sec. 04) skew toward this model.
  • Means giving up a real ownership percentage and taking on a shareholder base with associated rights and expectations.
  • Better fit if the long-term plan is expansion/scale-driven growth rather than steady per-facility cash flow.

Not resolved in this plan by design — worth a direct conversation with a securities attorney about which structure fits the actual long-term intent (steady multi-facility cash-flow business vs. a scale-and-exit growth story) before committing.

Mainvest is the clearest structural fit if the revenue-share path (Sec. 03) is chosen — it's specifically built for physical local businesses, requires no direct investor fee, and (notably) requires a new business to first raise at least $10,000 from 10 people in their own personal network before going live publicly. That's a real, useful forcing function: it's a built-in validation gate before the wider public ever sees the campaign, not just a hoop to jump through.

If equity is chosen instead, Wefunder carried the most 2024 Reg CF volume of the three general platforms, with the lowest all-in issuer cost of the group.

FND‑07

Timeline

  1. Legal setup — engage a securities attorney, form the entity if not already done, decide equity vs. revenue-share (Sec. 03).
  2. Financials — prepare self-certified, reviewed, or audited statements depending on the target tier (Sec. 02).
  3. If pursuing Mainvest specifically: raise the required $10,000 from 10 people in-network before the platform lists the campaign publicly (Sec. 04).
  4. Platform application & review — the funding portal reviews the offering (Form C, financials, campaign materials) before it goes live.
  5. Campaign runs — funds held in escrow (Sec. 04) until the raise closes; returned in full if the minimum isn't met.
  6. Close & deploy — funds released to the business; build-out of the first facility begins.
  7. Annual reporting — required for as long as the securities are outstanding (Sec. 02), not a one-time obligation.
FND‑08

Risks & ongoing obligations

  • Platform fees come off the top — 7.5–12% of whatever's raised (Sec. 04), before a dollar reaches the business.
  • Revenue-share payouts are a real, ongoing obligation if that structure is chosen — they continue until the note is satisfied, drawing on revenue regardless of how the rest of the business is performing in a given month.
  • Equity dilution is permanent if that structure is chosen — a real ownership stake goes to a public investor base, with the rights and expectations that come with it.
  • Annual reporting is a standing compliance cost, not a one-time filing — factor ongoing accounting/legal time into the business's overhead, not just the initial raise.
  • A failed raise (minimum not met) returns all funds — real prep work and legal cost can be spent without the facility ever getting built if the campaign doesn't hit its floor.
