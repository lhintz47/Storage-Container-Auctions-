ECON‑01

Cost model overview

Two different kinds of money, kept separate throughout this doc: CapEx — spent once to build a facility — and OpEx — spent every month to keep it running, regardless of how many auctions actually close. Revenue is per-transaction, tied to auctions actually completing. The business only works if per-transaction revenue at a realistic volume covers OpEx and pays back CapEx in a reasonable window — that's the question this document is actually trying to answer, not just list numbers.

Where a number below is real (sourced from a vendor or a market rate), it's marked. Where it's a placeholder standing in for a number nobody has quoted yet, that's marked too — this document is not ready to hand to an investor as-is (see the Funding Plan for that gap explicitly).

ECON‑05

Per-auction economics

Real winning bids on storage-style auctions vary enormously — reported figures range from under $100 to nearly $1,000, and one experienced buyer cited $1–$500 as typical for both live and online sales. There's no way to responsibly collapse that into a single "average" — so three scenarios instead, at $100 / $250 / $500 winning bids.

ECON‑06

Break-even & volume

Four bays, each cycling roughly weekly (business plan Sec. 04: ~2 days setup, ~3 days live, ~2 days removal). At full back-to-back utilization that's up to ~4 cycles/bay/month; realistically, for a new site still building bidder demand, call it 2–3 completed sales per bay per month — 8–12 completed auctions/month per facility, not the theoretical maximum.

ScenarioAuctions/moNet revenue/mo (mid-bid)
Conservative (2/bay)8~$424
Realistic (2.5/bay)10~$530
Optimistic (3/bay)12~$636
Partial break-even now possible, still not complete. Known OpEx (insurance + controller connectivity + interior cameras, Sec. 03) runs ~$107–214/month — comfortably below the $424–636/month net revenue range at realistic volume, which is an encouraging early signal. But property insurance, exterior-camera data, and facility labor are still unpriced, and labor in particular could plausibly be the largest OpEx line once it's real — there's still no honest way to call this "break-even confirmed." Filling in those three remaining lines is the highest-value next step, not a full re-model.
ECON‑07

Pricing strategy

The existing rates check out against the market

The business plan's 15% buyer's premium isn't an arbitrary number — real storage-auction operators charge winning-bid fees "usually based on a percentage of the winning bid, like 15%" per industry reporting (Sec. 10). That's a useful independent cross-check that the rate is in a normal, defensible range, not a guess that happens to also be a round number.

The real gap: facility hosting

Nowhere in the business, hardware, or funding plans is there a defined answer for what happens if the physical site is hosted by a third-party facility owner rather than owned outright by the platform operator. The business plan's Roles section allows for a Facility Owner distinct from the platform, but no fee or revenue-share arrangement between them is specified. This is worth resolving before approaching any real facility owner as a partner, not after.

Additional revenue, not yet priced

  • Featured/highlighted listings for sellers
  • Expedited or extended access windows
  • Photo/video inventory add-ons at intake

None of these are load-bearing for the core model — they're upside on top of premium + commission, worth pricing once the base model is validated, not before.

ECON‑08

Sensitivity & risk

  • Bid-value risk: the low-end scenario ($100 bids, $21 net) is a real possibility, not a tail case — reported winning bids run as low as "under $100." A facility that skews toward low-value sealed sections could sit well below the mid-case revenue this plan otherwise leans on.
  • Utilization risk: the 8–12/month volume assumption (Sec. 06) is itself unvalidated — a new site with no bidder base yet could run well below "conservative" for months before demand builds.
  • Unknown OpEx risk: cellular and labor costs, once real, could each individually be larger than the entire net revenue range modeled in Sec. 06 — this document cannot currently rule that out.
  • Payment risk: international cards or cross-border transfers add 1–2%+ on top of the base Stripe rate (Sec. 10) — a real cost if the bidder base isn't purely domestic.
